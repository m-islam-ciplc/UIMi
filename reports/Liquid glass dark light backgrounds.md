# Ship four CSS glass fields

**Ship two light and two dark CSS ambient fields—Mist and Porcelain for light, Slate and Graphite for dark—as selectable backgrounds in a Liquid Glass settings control.** Soft, low-frequency color pools over a deep or pale base give frosted panels chroma and structure to refract, while flat solids make glass invisible and busy photos make it muddy. For a workspace design console, a four-swatch curated set beats a wallpaper gallery: it mirrors how Slack and Discord keep appearance choices small, stays fully CSS-implementable with zero asset weight, and keeps contrast predictable under `backdrop-filter`. Pair Mist with light theme and Slate with dark by default; let Porcelain and Graphite cover warm-temperature preference. Treat these fields as the content-layer canvas under floating glass chrome—not as glass themselves—and raise fill opacity sharply on light scenes so milky panels do not dissolve into the sky.

## Soft color pools beat flat solids under frost

Premium glass reads as a **functional layer floating above content**, not as the content background itself. Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines place Liquid Glass on controls and navigation so underlying content can peek through while legibility holds; Regular glass blurs and adjusts luminosity, while Clear glass belongs only over visually rich media and needs a **35% dark dimming layer** when that media is bright ([Apple HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). WWDC25 frames Liquid Glass as lensing that adapts tint, shadows, and dynamic range to what sits behind it—shadows strengthen over text and weaken over solid light fields ([Meet Liquid Glass — WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).

Web practice converges on the same optical requirement: blur averages whatever is behind the pane, so a constant fill averages to itself and the panel collapses into a grey box. NN/g notes that glassmorphic elements stand out over gradients or complex backgrounds, with translucency controlled by opacity plus background blur ([NN/g: Glassmorphism](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glassmorphism/)). CSS authors recommend **blur roughly 12–30px**, **`saturate` around 120–180%** to restore chroma lost to averaging, and large soft color masses rather than high-frequency detail that mottles midtones under glyphs ([Gradiently](https://gradiently.design/guide/glassmorphism); [Farvist CSS guide](https://farvist.com/blog/glassmorphism-css-guide/)). Theme asymmetry matters: dark scenes tolerate fills near **6–25% white**, while light scenes need roughly **55% white fill** or a dark tint—about nine times more veil—so white-on-near-white does not fail ([Farvist CSS guide](https://farvist.com/blog/glassmorphism-css-guide/)). Avoid flat solids, stacked glass, neon chrome text, and frosted tables or long reading surfaces; reserve frost for floating chrome over controlled soft fields ([WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/acrylic)).

## Midnight slate and soft sky win the workspace shortlist

Among dark candidates researched for ops and design-kit UIs, **Midnight Slate Mesh** ranks first: a slate-950 base (`#0b1220` / `#020617`) with restrained blue blooms gives white rim lights something to catch without purple-glow marketing spectacle, matching production dark-glass tokens such as MengTo’s navy fills and inset highlights ([MengTo glass-dark-ui](https://www.ui-skills.com/skills/mengto/glass-dark-ui); [Superdesign — Glassmorphism](https://superdesign.dev/styles/glassmorphism)). **Soft Teal Aurora Charcoal** (`#0E1116` with a single low-alpha teal bloom) is the best “alive but restrained” alternate and explicitly rejects purple-blue AI gradients ([Standout — Aurora Glass](https://standoutmcp.io/directions/aurora-glass.md)). Obsidian warm charcoal suits dense data chrome when distraction must be minimal; Tahoe Shadow indigo–aqua approximates Apple’s Liquid Glass dark wallpaper mood but can read purple-ish on wide-gamut displays, so treat it as an optional theme rather than the kit default ([iClarified — Shadow wallpaper](https://www.iclarified.com/97855/apple-adds-new-shadow-halo-and-dusk-wallpapers-in-ios-26-beta-3-download)).

On the light side, **Soft Sky Wash**—`#dbeafe` → `#e0f2fe` → `#f0fdfa`—is the strongest product default: calm cool-to-mint shift, Apple Sky-adjacent, and already treated as a mandatory backdrop in light-only glass design systems that pair it with ~55% white cards, ~18px blur, and blue-tinted shadows ([Glass design system instructions](https://adp.itsnexor.cloud/api/design-systems/glass/instructions); [iGeeksBlog — iOS 26 wallpapers](https://www.igeeksblog.com/ios-26-wallpaper/)). **Cool Silver Daylight** (highlight `#f7f8fa` through mid greys `#e8eaee` / `#d8dde5`) is the best neutral chrome field for settings and editors, aligning with Silver material recipes aimed at dashboards ([Liquid Glass Studio — Silver](https://liquid-glass.weblocally.com/en/materials/silver/)). Warm daylight cream–sage and pastel corner meshes work optically but skew consumer or agency unless heavily desaturated; saturated purple–pink linear heroes and animated triple orbs are the light-mode failure modes to ban from a console ([UI Design Prompts — Glassmorphism](https://uidesignprompts.com/prompts/glassmorphism); [wigtn glassmorphism style guide](https://github.com/wigtn/wigtn-plugins/blob/main/plugins/wigtn-plugins/skills/design-system-reference/styles/glassmorphism.md)).

| Mode | Ship name | Optical ancestor | Core stops |
|------|-----------|------------------|------------|
| Light default | **Mist** | Soft Sky / cool silver wash | `#f4f7fb` → `#e2ebf5` → `#d5e0ec` with soft cool radials |
| Light alternate | **Porcelain** | Warm daylight muted | `#fbf7f2` → `#efe4d8` → `#e5d5c6` with peach-warm radials |
| Dark default | **Slate** | Midnight Slate Mesh | `#121820` → `#1a2430` → `#0e141c` with deep blue-gray blooms |
| Dark alternate | **Graphite** | Obsidian / warm charcoal | `#141210` → `#1c1816` → `#0c0b0a` with soft ember undertones |

These four compress the ranked research shortlists into material-named fields suitable for a settings control: cool vs warm within each luminance band, all CSS-only, no stock photos and no Apple system wallpaper binaries (those remain mood references only—Apple assets are not open-licensed design tokens) ([iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/97554/download-the-official-ios-26-wallpaper-for-iphone); [gradients.sh](https://gradients.sh/blog/pastel-gradient-css)).

## Four swatches, theme-paired, replace the single JPG

Productivity products keep appearance small: Discord ships a handful of named themes, Slack cut freeform theming down to constrained palettes, and Apple Wallpaper settings present curated families plus solids rather than infinite uploads as the primary path ([Discord Appearance](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/207260127-How-to-Change-Discord-Color-Themes-and-Customize-Appearance-Settings); [Slack Design](https://slack.design/articles/a-new-visual-language-for-slack/); [Apple Support: Wallpaper](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/wallpaper-settings-mchlp1103/mac)). Windows separates wallpaper from materials—Mica samples wallpaper once into a muted opaque base; Acrylic reveals wallpaper through transient frost but must not cover long-lived large backgrounds ([Mica](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/mica); [Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/acrylic)). For a Liquid Glass settings modal, mirror Slack/Discord: a **Background** section with a 2×2 radio swatch grid (or Light / Dark rows), 48–64px gradient previews, short material labels, live apply to the workspace behind the modal, and persistence of an ID such as `mist` \| `porcelain` \| `slate` \| `graphite`.

Appearance and background should stay **related but separable**. Default light theme to Mist and dark theme to Slate; when the user has not explicitly overridden the field, theme flips should auto-swap Mist ↔ Slate (and Porcelain ↔ Graphite if those were the prior defaults). After a deliberate pick, keep the chosen field until the user changes it—matching how macOS treats Dynamic Wallpapers that can follow Appearance while arbitrary photos often stay fixed ([Apple Support: Wallpaper](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/wallpaper-settings-mchlp1103/mac)). Filtering the grid by active theme (or warning on cross-luminance picks) reduces the main failure mode: light milky glass over a dark field or reverse without matching fill tokens. Implementation-wise, replace the hard-coded `assets/glass-bg.jpg` on `.uimi-main` with a CSS custom property `--uimi-glass-scene` driven by `data-uimi-glass-bg`, using stacked radials plus a linear base so blur samples continuous soft chroma rather than photographic high frequency.

## Contrast is measured after the composite, not the fill alone

WCAG 2.2 requires normal text at **≥4.5:1** and large text at **≥3:1** against the background *behind* the glyphs; patterned fields demand that ratio against the worst local sample that can scroll under a panel ([Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum)). Translucent fills must be alpha-composited over the scene before contrast is meaningful—comparing uncomposited black to white overstates the ratio ([AllColorTools](https://allcolortools.com/guides/alpha-compositing-contrast)). Opaque body text on adequately tinted glass survives; semi-transparent text fails first ([Lucky Graphics](https://lucky.graphics/learn/glassmorphism-text-contrast-backdrop-filter-support-2026/)).

Honor **Reduce Transparency** and CSS `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` by dropping `backdrop-filter`, raising glass fill toward opaque solids, and optionally clearing the decorative scene in favor of a solid theme background ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency); [Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-prefers-reduced-transparency); [Apple — Reduce Transparency](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-display-colors-iph3e2e1fb0/ios)). Browser support for the media query is not Baseline—Firefox and Safari may lack it—so ship an in-app “Reduce glass transparency” control rather than relying on the media query alone ([Can I Use](https://caniuse.com/wf-prefers-reduced-transparency)). Soft curated gradients make worst-case sampling stable; a single busy JPG under light frost does not.

## Conclusion

The shippable answer is not “find pretty wallpapers”—it is **four named CSS ambient fields** that satisfy Liquid Glass optics (large soft chroma over a luminance-correct base), console taste (cool defaults, warm alternates, no purple neon), and settings UX (small curated swatch set with theme-aware defaults). Mist and Slate become the light/dark system pair; Porcelain and Graphite absorb temperature preference without growing a wallpaper browser. What changes going forward is the implementation contract: glass chrome tokens stay separate from the scene, the scene is a data-attribute-driven CSS variable, and accessibility falls back to solids—so the settings control selects *fields under glass*, not another layer of frost.

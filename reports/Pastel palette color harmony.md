# Pastel hues hang together—with caveats
**Yes — the UIMi Pastel colors go together as a coherent soft multi-hue product theme**, not as a failed or accidental mix. Warm blush surfaces (`#fcf7f6` / `#fff5f7`) plus coral primary (`#e8a598`) form a peach atmosphere that glues sky info, mint success, butter warning, rose danger, and the chart accents into one family; that “warm wash + conventional multi-hue status” recipe matches how major soft UI kits (daisyUI pastel/valentine, Spectrum semantics, Catppuccin Latte) structure brand versus status. The set does **not** hang as a pure peach monochrome or a clean classical analogous scheme — and it should not. The real cohesion risks are role collisions inside the coral–rose band (primary ≈ danger ≈ chrome), mint’s under-chroma next to richer fills, pastel chips washing out on the warm canvas, and chrome’s light ink failing WCAG. Chromatic harmony is largely intact; semantic and accessibility distinguishability need targeted fixes, not a palette rewrite.

## Soft multi-hue matches product UI norms, not mono-peach mood boards
Professional product systems almost never keep every role inside one hue family. Adobe Spectrum maps informative → blue, positive → green, notice → orange, and negative → red independently of the brand accent ([Spectrum semantic color palette](https://opensource.adobe.com/spectrum-design-data/tokens/semantic-color-palette/)). Material Design 3 likewise treats success/warning-style meanings as static custom colors with their own hue ranges, separate from the dynamic brand scheme, and caps optional “harmonize” shifts at **15°** so error stays recognizably red ([Material: define new / static colors](https://m3.material.io/styles/color/advanced/define-new-colors); [MCU Blend.harmonize](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/blend/blend.ts)). Soft kits follow the same split: daisyUI’s `pastel`, `cupcake`, and `valentine` themes keep brand/base in one temperature family while info/success/warning/error span sky, green, butter, and coral/red ([daisyUI pastel object.js](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/daisyui@5.7.46/theme/pastel/object.js); [daisyUI valentine object.js](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/daisyui@5.7.46/theme/valentine/object.js)). Catppuccin Latte ships a full soft accent grid with apps mapping green→success, yellow→warning, red→danger ([Catppuccin palette](https://catppuccin.com/palette/)).

Against that baseline, UIMi Pastel’s coral brand + sky `#8fb4e0` + mint `#8fbf9a` + butter `#e0c06a` + rose `#e08a9e` is **structurally normal**, not muddled. It is the warm-blush twin of UIMi `lavender-mist` (violet wash + multi-hue status): same role architecture, different temperature. Pastel actually separates info from brand more clearly than lavender-mist, where info sits near the violet primary. Monotony would come from collapsing status into peach; muddle would come from equal-weight coral/mint/sky CTAs or primary≈danger collisions — not from using conventional soft status hues on a blush canvas ([ColorArchive – Pastel Color Palette](https://colorarchive.org/guides/pastel-color-palette/); [Pastel Pop](https://csscreme.com/palettes/pastel-pop); [Pastel Transfer](https://designmd.ai/chef/pastel-transfer)). ColorArchive’s warning that pink next to green can read like “Christmas decoration” applies to equal brand surfaces, not to green success badges on a blush app — a pattern Spectrum, daisyUI, and Catppuccin all ship.

## Lightness coheres; coral–rose–chrome is the harmony fracture
Measured HSL puts most semantic and chart fills in a soft band: **L ≈ 63.5–79%** (spread ~15.5) and **S ≈ 27–66%**, with mean L ≈ 69.5 and mean S ≈ 47. Practitioner pastel guidance targets high lightness and reduced chroma, often with shared value across swatches ([ColorArchive – Pastel Color Palette](https://colorarchive.org/guides/pastel-color-palette/); [DesignYourWay – Pastel Color Palettes](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/pastel-color-palettes/); [ColorUI – Pastel Color Palette Guide](https://colorui.io/blog/pastel-color-palette-guide)). UIMi’s fills read as “neo-pastels” — soft mid-lights with more chroma than dusty ultra-pastels — which is enough shared value for one product family. Saturation is the weak link: success mint at **S ≈ 27%** looks dusty beside coral/butter at **S ≈ 56–66%**, so the status row is one soft family with a quieter mint outlier rather than a tightly matched chorus.

Hue geometry tells a sharper story. Primary coral (~**10°**) and danger rose (~**346°**) are only ~**24°** apart, with near-matching L/S; chrome `#c97b8a` (~**348.5°**) sits ~**2.4°** from danger. Approximate Lab ΔE76 is ~**19** for primary vs danger and ~**7** for danger vs chrome — the latter is effectively the same rose family ([Coloristic – Semantic Status Color Tokens](https://coloristic.org/blog/semantic-status-color-tokens/); [Material Design 3 – Color roles (Error)](https://m3.material.io/styles/color/roles)). That cluster is aesthetically “harmonious” (analogous warm reds) but **role-hostile**: brand CTA, destructive status, and chrome all occupy one warm-red neighborhood. Material-style harmonization toward primary would make this worse; the palette needs de-harmonization of danger (and chrome) away from coral. Chrome labeled “navy” is chromatically dusty rose/mauve, so it reinforces the collision instead of supplying cool structure.

No single classical wheel model fits the full token set. Against coral, sky (~213°) and teal (~194°) behave as **near-complements**; coral + mint + sky have a loose split-complement flavor; coral–mint–lilac is **near-triadic** if butter/danger are ignored; butter (~44°) is simply analogous warm spine ([Adobe – Colour Wheel Combinations](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/design/discover/color-wheel.html); [Coolors color picker – harmonies](https://coolors.co/color-picker); [Itten Color Wheel](https://colortypekit.com/itten-color-wheel/)). The honest label is a **warm analogous brand spine + soft multi-hue / loose tetradic semantic and chart accents** — a pastel spectrum held by peach surfaces, not an Adobe “Analogous” lock of five status chips.

## Status fills stay readable on ink; canvas and chrome expose the soft-palette tax
Filled semantic buttons that pair Pastel fills with their dark inks clear WCAG 2.2 AA for normal text (**≥4.5:1**); primary, info, success, and warning also clear AAA (**≥7:1**). Local ratios: primary **8.40:1**, info **7.43:1**, success **7.63:1**, warning **7.55:1**, danger **6.93:1** (AA pass, AAA near-miss) ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum)). Page background versus those inks is strong (**~12.5–16.5:1**). The soft-palette tax appears elsewhere. Chrome `#c97b8a` with light ink `#fff8f6` is ~**2.99:1** — fails AA for normal text and, without rounding, fails the **3:1** large-text threshold too. Pastel fills versus `#fcf7f6` / `#fff5f7` sit around **1.7–2.4:1** (warning worst at ~**1.66:1**), so chips and pills can melt into the warm canvas and miss non-text contrast when fill alone must identify a control ([Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast); [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)).

Color-vision deficiency sharpens the same fracture. Under Machado-like deuteranopia simulation, primary and danger remain only moderately separated (approx. ΔE ~**14** after transform), while danger and chrome collapse further into the same soft mauve/gray chip family; CVD also strips danger’s “warm rose = error” identity into a desaturated blob ([NEI Color Blindness](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness); [Machado et al. CVD model](https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html); [Coblis](https://www.color-blindness.com/coblis-color-blindness-simulator/)). WCAG 1.4.1 and product accessibility guidance already require labels, icons, or patterns in addition to hue ([MDN – Using color wisely](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Colors/Using_color_wisely); [Section508.gov – Making Color Usage Accessible](https://www.section508.gov/create/making-color-usage-accessible/); [Apple HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Harmony and accessibility therefore agree: keep the multi-hue pastels, but never treat coral vs rose as self-explanatory status encoding.

## Keep the spectrum; separate roles and deepen edges
Cohesion should come from a **dominant warm blush wash**, matched pastel lightness/chroma, dark warm ink, and clearer primary-vs-danger separation — not from collapsing sky/mint/butter into peach ([Ramotion – App Design Color Palette Principles](https://www.ramotion.com/blog/app-design-color-palette/); [UXPin – Choose a Color Palette for UI Design](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/choose-color-pallete/); [DevPalettes – Pastel Color Guide](https://devpalettes.com/blog/pastel-color-guide/)). Pastel Pop and Pastel Transfer encode the usage rule that matters as much as hex math: **one primary CTA per view**; accents and status never compete as second primaries ([Pastel Pop](https://csscreme.com/palettes/pastel-pop); [Pastel Transfer](https://designmd.ai/chef/pastel-transfer)). Prefer peach surfaces and coral primary at large area; reserve sky/mint/butter/rose/lilac/teal for badges, charts, and sparse accents (60–30–10 style dominance).

Concrete retunes that preserve the soft multi-hue look: move danger cooler toward magenta (e.g. toward `#e08ab5` / `#d070a0`) so hue distance from coral approaches **≥~40°** and ΔE roughly doubles; either switch chrome to dark ink (`#2a1218` on `#c97b8a` ≈ **5.58:1**) or darken chrome to ~`#a85a6c` for light ink (**~4.61:1**) and break the danger twin; lift mint saturation toward the family mean (e.g. toward `#81cf93`); optionally deepen warning slightly (e.g. `#d4a84a`) and add same-hue borders so fills approach **3:1** object contrast without leaving pastel ([Coloristic – Semantic Status Color Tokens](https://coloristic.org/blog/semantic-status-color-tokens/); [ColorArchive – Why pastel palettes fail in production](https://colorarchive.org/notes/april-2026-pastel-palettes-production/)). Keep coral `#e8a598`, butter `#e0c06a`, sky `#8fb4e0`, lilac `#b8a4d9`, and the blush backgrounds — they already carry peach atmosphere and soft spectrum semantics. Suggested hexes are HSL/contrast engineering proposals; re-verify in WebAIM and APCA tools and with a deuteranopia pass before locking tokens ([WebAIM Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/); [APCA Contrast Calculator](https://apcacontrast.com/)).

## Conclusion
The Pastel theme’s colors **do go together** when judged as a product soft multi-hue system: peach canvas and coral primary supply temperature identity; sky, mint, butter, rose, and chart accents supply conventional, soft status and data chroma. The question “do they hang?” is therefore mostly yes — with the important caveat that **aesthetic harmony and role clarity diverge** inside the coral–rose–chrome cluster. Fixing that collision, evening mint’s chroma, restoring chrome text contrast, and giving pastel chips edges or depth will make the family feel more intentional without abandoning the soft spectrum that makes Pastel useful and industry-aligned. Going forward, treat Pastel as the warm twin of lavender-mist: same architecture, different wash — and measure success by whether brand, error, and chrome remain distinguishable under CVD and WCAG, not by whether every accent shares coral’s hue.
